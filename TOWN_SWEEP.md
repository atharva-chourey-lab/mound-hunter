# Mound Hunter — Town Sweep

Run 2026-11-17, on Atharva's commission: apply the Itbardi method —
*hunt the old literature for a textual fix, convert it to derived
coordinates with the arithmetic shown, then read the ground* — to the
towns around the Kasrawad–Maheshwar corridor, then a first pass at
the Chambal reach. The MP geology project is paused; this sweep is
archaeology / site-location only.

**Discipline (unchanged from v0/v1):**
- Open data only, no accounts, no purchases.
- The DEM detector is **unvalidated** (v1 calibration 0/2). DEM relief
  here is supporting evidence only; the primary method is textual +
  visual (Esri World Imagery chips in `outputs/town-sweep/`).
- Derived coordinates are flagged **TEXT-DERIVED, not surveyed**, with
  an honest uncertainty radius.
- **Candidates are places to look, never discovery claims.**
- Two names Atharva gave — *Dhawana* and *Anjar* — were unresolved
  before this sweep; they are handled in §E and §F with the
  identification question kept separate from the archaeology.

**Method, per town:** (1) textual sources with exact quotes;
(2) derived box — anchor, offset arithmetic, uncertainty;
(3) satellite / DEM reading of the box; (4) ranked look-places;
(5) what would settle it.

---

## A. Bagh — two genuine textual fixes around the town; no mound claimed

**Anchor discipline:** Garde (1927) fixes Bagh *town* at 22°22′N,
74°48′E = **22.3667°N, 74.8000°E** — ~4.9 km N of the caves point
(22.3227, 74.8062). Both sites below are relative to the town, so
the town point is the anchor. Conversions: 1 mi = 1.609 km;
1 km lat ≈ 0.009°, lon ≈ 0.0097°.

**Ground context (this sweep):** the caves point sits on a scrubby,
dissected plateau edge above a large meander of the Bagh river
(chip `bagh-caves-sat-z16.jpg`); GLO-30 relief at the point is
−11.6 m (cliff slope — the v0 negative control behaving as
designed). Natural rocky rises ring the whole area, which is why
v0's top candidates clustered here and why every Bagh look-place
below is textual first, terrain second.

### A1. Early-habitation brick foundations, SW of town — NEW

- **Source:** M. B. Garde, "Miscellaneous Notes on Bagh Caves," in
  Marshall et al., *The Bagh Caves in the Gwalior State* (1927):
  > "About a quarter of a mile to the South West of the town, on a
  > low hill, vestiges of early habitation are seen in the shape of
  > brick foundations."
  Traditionally attributed to Mordhaj / Mayuradhvaja.
- **Derived:** 0.25 mi = 0.402 km SW → 0.284 km S + 0.284 km W →
  lat 22.3667 − 0.0026 = 22.3641; lon 74.8000 − 0.0028 = 74.7972 →
  **22.364°N, 74.797°E, ±1.0 km** (anchor is rounded to the minute).
  TEXT-DERIVED.
- **Ground reading:** GLO-30 at the point: elev 241.0 m, relief
  −0.0 m, ruggedness 7.4; max relief within 300 m +20.7 m — low
  hill country, consistent with "on a low hill", nothing at the
  point itself. The discriminator in Garde's text is the **brick
  foundations**, not the hill: the hill is terrain, the foundations
  are the archaeology, and brick scatter is invisible at 30 m.
  Chip: `bagh-b1-brick-foundations-sat-z16.jpg` — the derived
  point's modern neighbourhood is a farmed/settled mosaic at the
  town's edge, with low scrub hills at the frame's W/NW; nothing
  is separable in imagery, and the ±1 km box (not the point) is
  the search unit. A ground visit is the only real test here.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM that the site is real (primary 1927
  ASI/Gwalior-State source, named tradition, diagnostic material);
  LOW–MEDIUM for the pin.
- **Look-place:** the low hill nearest **22.364, 74.797**, searched
  for brick scatter, not for a DEM bump.
- **What would settle it:** a ground visit (brick foundations are a
  surface find), or the Gwalior State archaeology files Garde drew on.

### A2. Second rock-cut cave group + Mahakalesvara temple, E of town — NEW

- **Source:** Garde 1927, same monograph:
  > "Half-a-mile to the East of the town on the bank of the Bagh
  > river are mere traces of a group of rock-cut caves distinct from
  > those which form the subject of the present monograph."
  > "Close by stands an 11th century temple of Siva, locally known
  > as Mahakalesvara."
- **Derived:** 0.5 mi = 0.805 km E → lon 74.8000 + 0.0078 = 74.8078 →
  **22.367°N, 74.808°E, ±1.0 km**. TEXT-DERIVED.
- **Ground reading:** GLO-30 at the point: elev 264.7 m, relief
  +12.9 m, ruggedness 4.6 — elevated river-bank ground E of town.
  "Mere traces" in 1927 means expect little surface signature; the
  temple, if extant, is the better ground marker. Chip:
  `bagh-b2-caves-east-sat-z16.jpg`.
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM (explicit, primary, expressly distinguished
  from the main caves).
- **What would settle it:** ground visit to the river bank E of
  town; the Mahakalesvara temple is findable in a way cave traces
  are not.

**Strictness note:** a full-text search of the opened 1927
monograph for "mound" returns no settlement-mound mention. A1 is a
foundation-on-a-low-hill site — it is **not** promoted to a mound
here. ASI's protected list for the locality carries only "Bagh
Caves" (N-MP-119); Dhar DCHB 2011 and district tourism pages add
nothing around the town.

## B. Maheshwar + Navdatoli

### B1. Navdatoli — the published coordinate is a town coordinate; the site is ~1 mile S

- **Sources (exact quotes):**
  - Stuckenrath, *Radiocarbon* 5 (1963), the excavators' own
    radiocarbon submission:
    > "Navdatoli (22° 11' N Lat, 75° 36' E Long), is on the S bank
    > of the Narmada River, opposite Maheshwar, in the Nimar
    > district, Madhya Pradesh, India."
  - Geedh & Nadgauda (2013):
    > "Maheshwar and Navdatoli (22° 11'N, 75° 36' E) are situated in
    > one of the fertile alluvial stretches and on the opposite
    > banks of the Narmada river."
  - Wikipedia's summary of Sankalia, Deo & Ansari (1971) / Ghosh
    (1958):
    > "Both the village and the site are located roughly a mile
    > south of the modern day town of Maheshwar."
    > "The archaeological site was defined by 4 distinct mounds"
- **Verdict on the published coordinate:** 22°11′N, 75°36′E is
  published *jointly* for Maheshwar and Navdatoli, two places on
  opposite banks ~1 mile apart — it cannot be a site fix for
  either. It is the town/joint coordinate, and it is also the
  `known_sites.json` point (22.1833, 75.6000). On the chip at that
  point (`maheshwar-navdatoli-listed-sat-z16.jpg`) the pin falls in
  cultivated fields beside a modern village and road — no mound
  signature, as expected for a coordinate that was never the site.
- **Derived:** anchor 22.1833, 75.6000 + 1 mile S (1.609 × 0.009 =
  0.0145°) → **22.169°N, 75.600°E, ±2.0 km**. TEXT-DERIVED.
  GLO-30 at the derived point: elev 139.0 m, relief −8.5 m — the
  point falls on sloping bank/bluff ground (the river reach), not
  on the mound field itself.
- **The one divergent pin:** Wikipedia carries 22.162, 75.583
  (~2.4 km S, ~1.8 km W of the joint pin), provenance unstated —
  corroboration, not authority. It lies ~1.9 km SW of the derived
  point, inside the ±2 km radius. Its DEM profile is the most
  mound-like of the three points: elev 155.7 m, **relief +4.8 m,
  max +10.4 m within 300 m** — a low rise on the south-bank plain.
  Its chip (`maheshwar-navdatoli-wiki-pin-sat-z16.jpg`) shows the
  pin at the south-bank edge of the wide Narmada, at a small
  village with Maheshwar's dense fabric directly across the river
  N and farmland S — the geography is exactly "S bank, opposite
  Maheshwar"; the mounds themselves are not separable from the
  village/field fabric in modern imagery.
  Chips: `maheshwar-navdatoli-derived-*`, `maheshwar-navdatoli-wiki-pin-*`.
- **Mound dimensions: NOT verified.** No opened source gives
  length/breadth/height for the four mounds; Sankalia, Deo & Ansari
  (1958, 1971) exist bibliographically but no full text was
  openable in this sweep — the dimensions, if anywhere, are there.
  (Tourism aggregators' "24 km from Maheshwar" was discarded as
  irreconcilable with every scholarly source.)
- **Look-place:** the low rise at/near **22.162, 75.583** first
  (best joint fit of text + terrain), then the derived point's
  ±2 km south-bank field.
- **What would settle it:** the 1958 excavation volume's site plan;
  a CORONA frame of the south bank opposite Maheshwar.

### B2. Maheshwar's own mound (Mahishmati) — real, but not a separable target

- IGNOU Unit 19: > "Maheshwar 70 miles south of Ujjain is the famous
  Mahishmati of the ancient texts. Located on the bank of the
  Narmada the historic mound rises to a height of 200 feet."
  West Nimar Gazetteer (1970): > "The foundations of Mahishmati,
  identified with modern Maheshwar, were laid around 1000 B. C."
- No offset exists to apply: the mound is in/under the town at the
  anchor itself (**22.183, 75.600, ±1.5 km**). The "200 feet"
  almost certainly includes the natural river bluff — do not read
  it as deposit depth. Confidence HIGH for the identification, LOW
  value as a new look-place.

### B3. Dhanbedi — a named western mound, quadrant only

- Sankalia, *JNSI* XVI (1954), on ~300 BC punch-marked coins:
  > "from a person who found them during the rainy season on one of
  > the western mounds, known as Dhanbedi, at Maheshwar."
- **No distance is given, so no honest derivation exists.** An
  indicative search centre 1.0 km W of the anchor (22.183, 75.590,
  ±2.0 km) is a placeholder quadrant, **not** a source figure —
  flagged as such wherever it is used. GLO-30 there: elev 166.7 m,
  relief +4.0 m, max +8.9 m within 300 m — low rises W of town,
  nothing separable. Confidence LOW–MEDIUM (Sankalia himself, 1954;
  name + direction only).
- **What would settle it:** the 1958 Maheshwar excavation volume,
  which should map the western mounds.

## C. Ujjain — Vaishya Tekri pinned; Kumhar Tekri boxed

Town anchor (verified from a source opened in this sweep —
kaalapanchang.com): **23.1765°N, 75.7885°E**. Conversions used:
1 km latitude ≈ 0.009°, 1 km longitude ≈ 0.0095° at 23°N.

### C1. Vaishya Tekri — the sweep's cleanest new pin

**Textual sources (exact quotes):**

- ASI, *List of Centrally Protected Monuments/Sites* (Jan 2024):
  > "164. Ancient Mound (Vaishya Tekri) — Undasa — Ujjain"
  The ASI gives the locality (**Undasa**) and no coordinates.
- Hindi source compiled on the Jatland Bhairogarh page (from ASI
  Bhopal / Hindi sources):
  > "उज्जैन शहर से लगभग 5-6 किलो मीटर दूरी पर उज्जैन से मक्सी रोड पर ग्राम कानीपुरा के पास स्थित पीलिया खाल नाले पर दो बौद्ध स्तूप स्थित हैं।"
  > ("About 5–6 km from Ujjain city, on the Ujjain–Maksi road, near
  > Kanipura village, on the Piliya Khal nala, two Buddhist stupas
  > are situated.")
  > "जिनकी ऊचाई 100 और ब्यास लगभग 350 फीट है।" — height 100 ft,
  > diameter about 350 ft.
- Ujjain City Development Plan (JNNURM):
  > "Candpajjota's mother Gopal Mata is said to have got the stupa at
  > Kanchanvan (Kanipura) constructed under the influence of
  > Mahakaccayana. This site is known today as Vaishya Tekri."
  > "This stupa is said to have a diameter of 110 meters and a height
  > of 32 meters." (110 m ≈ 361 ft; 32 m ≈ 105 ft — the same mound as
  > the 350 ft × 100 ft tradition.)
- Skilling 2011, as summarised in Aug 2026 coverage: Vaishya Tekri
  "measures about 350 feet at its base and 100 feet high"; "Nearby
  stands a smaller structure called Kumbhar (or Kumhar) Tekri."
- The Aug–Oct 2026 ASI/MP re-excavation and GPR reports opened in
  this sweep give **no village, distance, or coordinates** — only
  "in / near Ujjain". The precise fix comes from the older sources
  above, not the new press.

**Published coordinates:** none in Wikipedia / Wikidata / GeoNames.
Two Meta/Facebook place pins (search-listing provenance, page open
blocked — treat accordingly): 23.212587, 75.814755 and 23.211373,
75.817402 — 0.31 km apart.

**Derived fix (arithmetic):** mean of the two pins = **23.2120°N,
75.8161°E**. Check against the anchor: Δlat 0.03548° = 3.94 km N;
Δlon 0.02758° = 2.90 km E; straight-line **4.9 km NE** of the town
anchor — fully consistent with "5–6 km, Maksi road, near Kanipura".
**Uncertainty ±0.5 km. Confidence HIGH for a literature-derived
fix** (ASI locality + Hindi textual fix + CDP identification + two
map pins converge within ~0.3 km). TEXT-DERIVED, not surveyed.

**Refined centre — the mound is visible, and the DEM peak lands on
it.** The z16 chip at the pin (`ujjain-vaishya-tekri-sat-z16.jpg`)
shows, SE of the pin, a large square enclosure outline (the
rectangular moat the 2026 reports describe) with a dark circular
mound at its centre, in a bare square plot amid farmland. Chip
coordinates must be converted through the tile grid (the chip
snaps to a tile window, so the drawn crosshair is the tile centre,
not the pin — the v0 caveat); done properly, the visual mound
centre converts to **23.21134°N, 75.81739°E**. The GLO-30 peak in
the same field — **elevation 512.3 m, relief +22.4 m, the maximum
of the whole 1.5 km window** — sits at **23.21153°N, 75.81736°E**.
**Visual centre and DEM peak agree within ~21 m.** At the refined
centre (**23.2114°N, 75.8174°E**) the DEM reads relief **+22.4 m at
the point itself** (the texts claim ~32 m of height; a 450 m-
smoothed 30 m DEM under-reads a ~110 m-wide mound, in the right
direction). The mound's dark patch in the chip measures ~100 m
across — the texts' 110 m diameter. **Refined uncertainty ±0.15 km**
— the tightest fix in this sweep, because here the textual hunt
ends at a mound that is still standing and visible. (A smaller
circular rise, relief +6.0 m at its point, sits ~0.95 km WNW at
23.21305, 75.80816 — unnamed in any source opened; recorded as a
look-place only, not identified with any tekri.)

**Look-place:** the mound itself, **23.2114, 75.8174** — centre of
the square enclosure SE of the pin-mean, on the Piliya Khal nala
line NE of Ujjain on the Maksi road.

**What would settle it:** the ASI's own 2026 excavation/GPR records
will carry a surveyed location; a CORONA frame would show the
mound's undisturbed 1960s outline and the rectangular moat the 2026
reports describe.

### C2. Kumhar Tekri — boxed, not pinned

**Textual sources:**
- ASI list: > "165. Ancient Mound (Kumbhar Tekri) — Undasa — Ujjain"
  (same locality as Vaishya Tekri).
- Hindi source (same page as above):
  > "वेश्या टेकरी के पूर्ब की ओर लगभग 1 किलोमीटर की दूरी पर स्थित कुम्हार-टेकरी नामक एक अन्य टीला था। यह टीला 220 फीट लंबा 110 फीट चौड़ा और 15 फीट ऊंचा था।"
  > ("About 1 km to the **east** of Vaishya Tekri was another mound
  > called Kumhar-Tekri; 220 ft long, 110 ft wide, 15 ft high.")
- TOI (4 Sep 2026), via Young INTACH: Vaishya Tekri is "situated
  roughly 2 km from Kumhar Tekri" — **no direction given**, and the
  distance conflicts with the Hindi source's 1 km. Both cannot be
  centre-to-centre figures.

**Derived box:** from the Vaishya pin, 1 km east → 23.2120,
75.8256; 2 km east → 23.2120, 75.8351. **Search centre 23.212°N,
75.830°E, uncertainty ±1.5 km. Confidence LOW–MEDIUM** (locality and
eastward relation sourced; distance contradicted; no published pin).
GLO-30 at the search centre is flat (relief at point −0.7 m, max
+7.8 m within 300 m) — nothing at the centre itself, which is what a
±1.5 km box means; the box, not the point, is the product.

**What would settle it:** a single ASI site plan or a CORONA frame
covering both tekris; the 1938–39 excavation (42 skeletons) records.

### C3. Garhkalika / ancient Ujjayani — an area, not a mound pin

- Ujjain CDP: > "The ancient city of Ujjayani is said to have existed
  about 6 km. to the north of today's Ujjain. Garhkalika as it is
  known today…" > "The ancient mounds in this area still yield
  ancient artifacts and remains. The excavations have exposed the mud
  fortifications of a citadel, datable from Circa 700–500 B.C."
- Derived: 6 km N of anchor → **23.231°N, 75.789°E, ±2.0 km,
  confidence MEDIUM-LOW**. GLO-30 there is river-plain flat (relief
  −3.1 m) — this is a settlement-area fix near the Kshipra, not a
  mound centre, and is recorded as such.
- Related ASI entry: > "163. Ancient Mound — Bhairogarh — Ujjain",
  with Bhairogarh village "3 km north of Ujjain" → derived 23.204°N,
  75.789°E ±1.5–2 km, confidence LOW; may not be separable from the
  Garhkalika area on text alone.

**What would settle it:** published excavation plans for the
Garhkalika/Bhairogarh citadel area with coordinates.

## D. Kasrawad — the Itbardi box, read on the ground; and a new label west of it

### D1. The box and its textual basis (from V1_SCOUTING §1)

Diskalkar 1949, p. 1 (the sole excavation publication; archive.org
DLI 2015.99782):
> "At a distance of **about three miles to the south of Kasrawad** …
> lies a site called Itbardi or mound of bricks" — "a rocky uneven
> tableland measuring about 300 ft. in length … situated half way on
> a hillock full of dwarf jungle trees. It is all hilly to its south
> and on the other sides there is no habitation at a distance of less
> than two or three miles."

Corroborated independently by Paranjpe, *A Short Guide Book to
the Central Museum, Indore* (1961):
> "At a distance of about three miles to the south of Kasrawad
> Tehsil lies a … site called Itbardi or mound of bricks."
(And the West Nimar Gazetteer, 1970, at locality level: "Kasrawad
contains early Buddhist relics.")

Derived centre (3 miles = 4.8 km due south of the town point
22.1333°N, 75.6167°E): **22.090°N, 75.617°E, ±1.5 km, TEXT-DERIVED**.
No published source gives lat/lon for the mound; the ASI list
carries it as N-MP-209, "Excavated site, Kasrawad", locality only.

### D2. Programmatic reading of the box (Esri chips + GLO-30)

This sweep's own reading (chips `kasrawad-itbardi-*-sat-z*.jpg` in
`outputs/town-sweep/`; GLO-30 tile N22_00_E075_00, v0/v1 relief
recipe). The browser Google Maps reading of the same box is being
done separately by the main chat; where the two agree, that is
noted.

- **Centre (22.090, 75.617):** elev 215.8 m, relief +9.48 m
  (v1 measurement, reproduced in scouting) — hillock class, terrain
  as Diskalkar describes: open rocky scrub tableland, farmland
  south, no close habitation *at the centre pixel*.
- **West scrub upland (~22.090–22.092, 75.608–75.612)** — the
  browser reading's strongest terrain match, cross-checked here:
  at 22.091, 75.610 the DEM gives **elev 234.3 m, relief +21.4 m,
  ruggedness 12.3** — the crest belt of the upland, ~0.7 km west of
  centre, no habitation in the chips. At the browser reading's
  sub-point **22.088, 75.611** (southern foot, above a dark nullah,
  farmland below): elev 215.3 m, relief +3.4 m — ~19 m below the
  crest, i.e. exactly the **"halfway up a hillock"** profile the
  text describes, on the upland's southern slope.
  The z16 chip at the sub-point (`kasrawad-west-upland-foot-sat-z16.jpg`)
  shows exactly this ground: a broad open scrub upland — mottled
  grey-brown rocky surface with dwarf scrub, Diskalkar's "hillock
  full of dwarf jungle trees" in modern dress — farmland patchwork
  N and S, the highway and Chhoti Kasrawad ~1 km E at the frame
  edge, a dark nullah line just S of the point with fields below
  it, and **no habitation anywhere on the upland**. No mound
  signature is resolvable in the chip; the match is terrain-class,
  and is stated as no more than that.
- **v1's open-tableland candidates, re-read:** cand-06
  (22.08728, 75.61421; Ø 232 m, +7.7 m, circ. 0.89, 0.42 km W of
  centre) sits on the upland's SE foot — same terrain family as the
  sub-point above. Cand-01 (22.09653, 75.60494; Ø 112 m, +9.4 m,
  1.43 km NW) sits toward the upland's NW rim — see D3.
- **NE cluster — downgraded, now with a name.** The browser reading
  confirms what v1's chips suggested: **Chhoti Kasrawad village
  straddles the highway 0.6–0.9 km NE/E of centre**. GLO-30 at the
  cluster point (22.09423, 75.62069): elev 208.4 m, relief +12.2 m —
  strong relief, but on disturbed settlement-edge ground. Diskalkar's
  "no habitation at a distance of less than two or three miles"
  sits awkwardly with a village 0.6 km away, which **favours the
  west upland over both the exact centre and the NE cluster** as
  the reading of his fix. (Caveat: habitation in 1949 ≠ habitation
  in 2026; this is a soft argument, stated as one.)

**Ranked look-places in/near the box:**
1. **22.088, 75.611** — southern foot of the west upland, "halfway
   up" profile, above the nullah, no habitation. Best joint fit to
   text + terrain.
2. **22.08728, 75.61421** (v1 cand-06) — open tableland W of centre,
   the strongest DEM-bump candidate on undisturbed ground.
3. **22.091, 75.610** — upland crest belt; if the tableland is on
   the crest rather than the slope, it is here.
4. The NE cluster (22.092–22.094, 75.621) — **downgraded**:
   village-edge disturbance, and the habitation argument above.

### D3. NEW LEAD — Google Maps label "Boudh stup", 22.0943643, 75.6027483

Folded in from the main chat's Google Maps reading of the box
(2026-11-17), with the same no-claim discipline:

- **What it is:** a Google Maps labelled place, "Boudh stup"
  (Tourist attraction, 2 reviews), at **22.0943643°N, 75.6027483°E**
  — **1.55 km WNW of the derived centre, just OUTSIDE the ±1.5 km
  box**, on the northern rim of the same west scrub upland, south of
  farmland. Its Google panel photo shows a grassy rounded hillock
  with people standing on it. Google place labels are
  user-contributed; provenance and date of the label are unknown.
- **It is NOT identified as Itbardi in any source found.** Textual
  check run in this sweep (web searches: "Boudh stup" Kasrawad;
  Sawda/Savda + Kasrawad + stupa; Itbardi location literature): **no
  published or web source found mentions a stupa NW of Kasrawad
  town, or near Savda/Sawda, other than Itbardi itself.** Every
  Kasrawad-stupa reference found (Diskalkar 1949; Paranjpe 1961;
  modern exam/summary literature) describes the single Kasrawad
  site — "6 km from Maheshwar", "banks of the Narmada", Karandikar
  1936, "Buddha cairn" finds — i.e. Itbardi, fixed only as "about
  three miles south of Kasrawad". A village **Savda** does exist in
  Kasrawad tehsil (Census/village listings), but no source opened
  places it, or any stupa, at the Boudh stup pin; the one located
  Savda/Sabda listing (22.0315°N, 75.6382°E) is ~7 km SSE of the pin.
- **How it relates to the box — stated plainly:** outside the box,
  on the same upland system, at the upland's NW rim — close to v1
  cand-01's pale rocky tableland patch (22.09653, 75.60494, 0.28 km
  NE of the pin). Relative to the **town**, the pin is 4.33 km S +
  1.44 km W = **4.56 km (2.83 miles) SSW of the town point** —
  which is *also* inside the tolerance of Diskalkar's loose "about
  three miles to the south". **That is precisely the problem:** the
  1949 fix is too loose to discriminate between the derived centre,
  the west-upland foot, and this pin, 1.55 km apart. The pin is a
  **competing look-place, not an identification** — possibilities
  include (a) local memory of the Itbardi mound under a generic
  name, (b) a different, unrecorded stupa mound on the same upland,
  (c) a natural hillock locally called a stupa. The literature
  cannot currently separate these.
- **Ground reading (this sweep, GLO-30 + Esri chip):** at the pin,
  elev **216.8 m, relief at point +2.0 m, ruggedness 14.2** (rocky);
  max relief within 300 m **+26.5 m**, max elev 243.5 m (the upland
  crest). The pin sits at the **northern foot/rim** of the upland,
  below the crest — again a "halfway up / foot of hillock" position
  rather than a crest position. The z16 chip
  (`kasrawad-boudh-stup-sat-z16.jpg`) shows the pin in open mottled
  scrub with **no habitation near it**, farmland beginning to the
  north — and, ~0.3 km NE of the pin, the distinct pale oval
  tableland patch that v1's cand-01 chip also caught: the single
  most tableland-like feature in this part of the upland. No
  circular mound signature is resolvable at the pin itself in
  modern imagery — stated plainly.

**What would settle the whole Kasrawad question:** a CORONA KH-4B
frame over the upland (the mound as it stood before modern
disturbance, at 1.8 m), or one ground visit with a GPS to the three
look-places (D2 #1, D2 #2, D3 pin) checking for brick scatter —
Diskalkar's site was a *brick* mound, and brick debris is the
discriminator no imagery at 30 m can supply.

## F. Anjar / Anjad — no regional Anjar; Anjad run as the unconfirmed candidate

**Identification verdict:** the only verified Anjar is the town in
Kutch, Gujarat (23°08′N, 70°01′E; GeoNames 1278573). **No Anjar
exists in Madhya Pradesh / the Narmada valley** in GeoNames
MP-scoped search, the Census 2011 Barwani PCA village list, Maps of
India, or VillageInfo — the only "Anjar-" string in Barwani's
Census list is **Anjarada** (Pati tehsil), a different name. One
pincode aggregator's "BADWANI ANJAR" is, in its own body, Anjad S.O.
— a mislabel, not evidence. **No temple or place named "Anjar
Bhawani" or "Anjad Bhawani" was found anywhere.** Anjad's goddess
temple in the sources is Nagari Mata, not Bhawani.

**Therefore: Anjad, Barwani (22.0413°N, 75.0503°E — corroborated by
Wikipedia at 22°02′N, 75°03′E, elev ~151 m) remains the only
plausible regional candidate for Atharva's "Anjar", and everything
below is run on Anjad with the identification flagged UNCONFIRMED
throughout.**

### F2. The only Anjad-specific historic item — Nagari Mata temple (not a mound)

  Satpura near Anjad. There is an ancient temple of Badi Mata and

- DCHB Barwani 2011, Brief History: > "The excavation carried out

- A village **Pipari** (Barwani block) is listed at 22.0529652°N,

  148.9 m, relief +0.6 m, max +1.9 m within 300 m) — plausible
  terrain for a buried river-bank settlement, and nothing visible,
  which is what a 5,000-year-old excavated river-bank site under

- **What would settle Anjad:** the ASI Prehistory Branch's Pipri

## G. Chambal (phase 2) — first pass, archaeology only

_First pass, clearly labelled as such. Anchors verified from an
opened page (DistanceCalculator): Bhind 26.5667°N, 78.7873°E;
Morena 26.5029°N, 78.0016°E. Village anchors are each from a
MapsOfIndia page opened in this pass. Conversions: 1 km lat ≈
0.009°, lon ≈ 0.0096° at 26.5°N. The MP geology project is paused —
nothing below is geology; the Chambal work here is site-location
only._

**Headline of the pass:** the famous Chambal temple sites all carry
real site coordinates now, so they are calibration controls, not
targets. The pass found **one genuinely lost text-only site —
Samantukhera, a Gupta-period brick town on the Chambal** — and one
small text-only mound, Sujawa at Kutwar.

### G1. Samantukhera — ancient brick town, right bank of the Chambal ★ best text-only target

- **Source:** Archaeological Department, Gwalior State, *Annual
  Administration Report 1940-41*, ¶85 + Appendix E (archive.org
  IGNCA 27334; OCR garbles the name, reading unambiguous in context):
  > "Samantukhera is situated about 4 miles to the north-west of
  > Bagchini and about a mile north of Gudha[j] on the right bank
  > of the Chambal and has been badly dissected by its ravines."
  > "Ruins of an ancient brick town now cut up into ravines on the
  > banks of the Chambal about a mile to the N. W. of village Gadha."
  > "That the site dates from the Gupta period can be judged from
  > the large size bricks in the ruins of what appears to be a city
  > fortification wall"
  Stone temple ruins stood a short distance S of the brick
  fortifications.
- **Published coordinates: none found** — a genuinely text-only site.
- **The two fixes conflict (shown, not smoothed):**
  - From Gudha Chambal (26.53130169°N, 77.77720583°E), 1 mi N:
    26.5313 + 0.0145 = **26.546, 77.777**.
  - From Bagchini (26.4652°N, 77.8040°E), 4 mi NW (components
    4.55 km N + 4.55 km W): **26.506, 77.760**.
  - The derivations sit ~4.7 km apart (one distance is likely a
    cart-track, not a straight line). **Proposed search point =
    midpoint 26.526°N, 77.769°E, uncertainty ~4 km. Confidence LOW.**
- **Ground reading (this sweep, GLO-30 tile N26_00_E077_00):** the
  Gudha-fix point reads **relief +4.7 m, ruggedness 8.7** —
  ravine-dissected ground, matching the text's terrain exactly;
  the midpoint reads relief −2.7 m, ruggedness 3.9 (flatter,
  less ravine-like). On terrain fit, the **Gudha fix is the better
  end of the box**. Its chip
  (`chambal-samantukhera-gudha-fix-sat-z16.jpg`) shows the point
  on the bluff line of the wide, sandbarred Chambal, with
  ravine-cut scrub country S of the bank and farmland patches
  among the ravines — the 1941 terrain description, verbatim in
  landscape form. Chips: `chambal-samantukhera-gudha-fix-*`,
  `chambal-samantukhera-midpoint-*`. The right-bank + ravine
  constraint is the narrowing tool: in a fuller pass, walk the
  derived segment along the Chambal's right bank and look for
  brick scatter in ravine sections — ravines are where a dissected
  brick town shows itself.
- **What would settle it:** the Gwalior report's own site map if
  one exists in the full report; CORONA (ravine edges in 1960s
  imagery); a ground traverse of the right-bank segment.

### G2. Kutwar (Kotwal) high mound + Sujawa mound, Asan bank

- **Sources:** Gwalior State *Annual Report 1940-41*, ¶84 +
  Appendix E; Garde, *Archaeology in Gwalior* (1934):
  > "The ancient site of Kutwar identified with Kantipuri, one of
  > the three capitals of the Nagas, is a high mound, part of which
  > is occupied by the existing village and another part has been
  > submerged in the Irrigation Dam on the Asan."
  > "A mound on the river bank known Suijawa about a mile to the
  > N. E. of village, possible site for excavation."
  > Garde, on Padhavli: "the neighbouring town of Kutwal (6 miles
  > north-west) or ancient Kantipuri one of the three capitals of
  > the Nagas" — cross-check: 6 mi NW of Padhavli lands ~2 km from
  > Kotwal village. Consistent.
- **Published coordinates:** only the *village* (Kotwal,
  26.47353195°N, 78.12944671°E) — the main mound is effectively
  published as the town's coordinate, because the village sits on it.
- **Derived:** main mound = **26.474, 78.129** (±1 km). Sujawa:
  1 mi NE → components 1.138 km N + 1.138 km E → **26.484, 78.140,
  ±1.5 km**. Confidence MEDIUM.
- **Ground reading:** Kutwar point is flat in the DEM (relief
  +0.1 m — a mound occupied by a village and partly submerged reads
  as no mound at 30 m, as expected). **Sujawa's derived point
  carries relief +8.0 m at the point, max +9.2 m within 300 m** —
  a genuine low bump on the Asan bank at exactly the derived
  position; supporting evidence only, and the cleanest DEM reading
  of any text-only target in this sweep. Chips:
  `chambal-kutwar-mound-*`, `chambal-sujawa-mound-*` — the Sujawa
  chip shows the point at the S edge of a scrub upland E of Kotwal
  village, with the Asan irrigation reservoir (the dam of the 1941
  text) filling the frame's W edge and farmland S.
- **Look-place:** **26.484, 78.140** (Sujawa). **What would settle
  it:** a ground visit; Sujawa was flagged "possible site for
  excavation" in 1941 and, so far as this pass found, never dug.

### G3. Controls — sites where the textual fix can be graded against a real coordinate

These calibrate the method itself, which is why they are in a
site-location report at all:

- **Bateshwar (from Padhavli):** Garde (1934): > "The Bhutesvara or
  Batesvara Valley is about three quarters of a mile to the
  south-west of Padhavli". Derived from the Padavli temple point
  (UNESCO 26.4313, 78.2024): SW 0.75 mi → **26.424, 78.194** —
  **~0.3 km from UNESCO's Bateshwar site coordinate (26.4257,
  78.1962)**. The best textual fix in the pass. DEM at the published
  point: relief −8.0 m (a valley floor), max +33.3 m within 300 m —
  temples in a valley among hills, as described.
- **Kakanmath (from Sihoniya):** Garde: > "Suhania is 20 miles
  south-east of Morena Station"; > "Kakanmadh … is situated about
  2 miles to the north-west of the village." Derived: **26.594,
  78.243** vs UNESCO's site coordinate **26.585, 78.249** — ~1.1 km
  apart, inside the error budget. **Warning:** Garde's Morena
  bearing fails — Sihoniya is actually ~28.6 km **ENE** of Morena,
  not SE; his distance was roughly right, his direction was not.
  Old textual fixes can be wrong in bearing even from good authors.
- **Ater Fort (from Bhind):** modern text: > "Ater Fort is a fort
  located 35 km (22 mi) west of Bhind". Derived due-W point:
  26.567, 78.451. The real fort (published site coordinate 26.748,
  78.637) is **~28 km NE of the derived point** — "35 km west" is a
  road distance and the true bearing is NW. Textual-only error
  would be 15+ km. DEM at the real fort: relief +22.9 m (a fort on
  high ground — the control behaves).

**Calibration lesson for the whole sweep:** short offsets from a
named village (0.75–2 miles) landed within 0.3–1.1 km of surveyed
coordinates; long or road distances (20–35 km) carried bearing
errors of tens of km. That is exactly the pattern the corridor
sections assume — Itbardi's 3-mile offset is in the trustworthy
class; its remaining uncertainty is the anchor and the word
"about", not the method.

**Dead ends (checked, nothing derivable):** Cunningham's *Four
Reports* vol. II body text for Kutwar/Subaniya (contents confirm
the sections; page OCR not retrievable in this pass — Puratattva's
citation of pp. 399–401 was used instead); Chakrabarti's late-
Harappan Asan-bank sites Khudai and Bassaiya (names + river only,
no fix); Mitawali/Chausath Yogini (modern travel distances only);
Ambah/Porsa/Sabalgarh as anchors (no mound fix tied to them);
Bhind district material beyond Ater; a Shastri lead on a Suhania
mound "south-west of village" seen in snippets but not opened, so
no quote is claimed for it.

**Overall read:** promising for a fuller pass **only** on
Samantukhera + Sujawa; the rest of the reach's famous sites are
already coordinate-resolved and serve as controls.
